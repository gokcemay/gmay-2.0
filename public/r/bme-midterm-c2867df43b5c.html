Five things to work on

Q36 · 9% correct

Owned, earned and paid channels

Thirty-eight of forty-seven chose option A. Option A is the definition of an owned channel: one the firm controls, such as its own site or mailing list.

An earned channel is attention the firm receives without paying directly for the placement: press coverage, a user's own post, a referral. A paid channel is placement bought with money. Channel strategy does not work until the three are separated.

Q2 · 9% correct

Maslow, a second time

The service offers badges, rankings and an annual list, and offers no messaging, no groups and no way to find other users. No social bond is formed; what is offered is visibility and recognition. That is the esteem level.

Nineteen chose “belonging” and fourteen chose “self-actualisation”. The Maslow question on the first quiz also stopped at 18%. The same gap has now appeared twice: the order of the levels is memorised, but placing a concrete example on the right level is not.

Week 1 · Maslow
Q18 · 17% correct

What a discovery interview is for

“The purpose of a customer discovery interview is to persuade the customer of the idea” is false. The purpose is to learn, not to persuade; the moment you start persuading, what you hear is politeness rather than evidence.

The answers spread almost evenly across the five options (11/8/8/7/13), which means most of the class guessed. The other three statements were true: neutral prompts, a pattern across several conversations, a workaround the customer built.

Week 3 · Customer discovery
Q37 · 28% correct

Sector tiers

Knowledge-based services sit in the quaternary tier. The answers spread across four options, so the order of the terms is not known.

The sequence is: primary for raw materials, secondary for manufacturing, tertiary for services, quaternary for knowledge and R&D, quinary for top-level decision-making.

Week 4 · Sector classification
Q24, Q28, Q29 · all three at 34%

Failing to reject one false statement

In Q24 the false statement was “for an SME, growth in headcount is a better health measure than gross margin”. Q28 and Q29 asked which statements were incorrect rather than correct, and two thirds of the class lost the thread on that reversal.

The error pattern is the same in all three: once three of the four statements are true, the fourth is treated as true too and “all of them” gets marked.

Method · Weeks 5 and 6

What to do before the final

  1. On I–IV questions, mark each of the four statements T or F before you look at the options. Only then read the options. Most of the marks lost on this paper were lost here.
  2. Read the stem twice to see whether it asks which statements are incorrect. Two questions on this paper were reversed and both stopped at 34%.
  3. Stop at absolutes: “always”, “never”, “entirely”, “is sufficient on its own”. Most of the false statements on this paper used that pattern.
  4. Go back through the Week 3 and Week 5 slides. They are the two weakest weeks on content, and three of the five points above come from them.
  5. The class is in good shape on case questions (79%). Carry that method — work out what is being asked, then eliminate — across to the statement questions.
