You know the material. What is missing is the habit of judging four statements one at a time. The most common error on those questions is always the same: marking “all four”. One false statement goes unnoticed, and the answer chosen from five options is the wrong one.

Four things to work on

Question 12 · 18% correct

The order of Maslow's hierarchy

The weakest question on the paper. Physiological needs sit at the base of the pyramid; only 8 of 44 marked it. Eighteen said “safety”, nine said “esteem”.

The answers spread almost evenly across all five options, which means the order is not settled in memory. The base is physiological, then safety, belonging, esteem and self-actualisation.

Week 1 · the Maslow slide
Questions 8 and 10 · 14 students in each

The “all four are correct” trap

One statement in question 8 read: “Judging each idea as soon as it appears is recommended, because it saves time.” That is false; the rule in brainstorming is quantity first, judgment later. Question 10 offered “An MVP is the complete product, released to every customer at the same time.” Also false; an MVP is the smallest testable slice of value.

In both questions 14 students took the false statement as true and answered “I, II, III and IV”.

Method: mark each statement true or false before you read the options
Question 7 · 52% correct

How entrepreneurship and management divide the work

The statement “Innovation creates value, whereas management is what delivers it” is correct; it is the course's own definition. Fourteen students ruled it out as false.

The two are complementary, not competing: the entrepreneur finds and shapes the opportunity, management coordinates people and resources and produces the result. In a small firm one person often does both, but the function does not disappear.

Week 2 · Entrepreneurship and Management
Question 14 · 66%  ·  Question 9 · 52%

Management functions and classification lenses

In question 14, eight students chose “define, measure, analyse, improve” (DMAIC) instead of POLC; that is the Six Sigma cycle. The answer is plan, organise, lead, control.

In question 9, eight students missed the false statement claiming that a digital, asset-light business and a brick-and-mortar business have identical unit economics.

Weeks 2 and 4

What to do before the midterm

  1. Review the four points above from the relevant slides. About an hour of work in total.
  2. On I–IV questions, mark each of the four statements true or false before you read the options. Most of the points lost on this quiz were lost there.
  3. When a statement contains an absolute word such as “always”, “never” or “every”, read it twice; three of the false statements on this paper used that pattern.
  4. The class is already strong on case questions. Carry that method, working out what is being asked and then eliminating options, over to the statement questions.
